tiny photo booth

A wall-mounted photo strip machine for dive bars. Press start. Pay five bucks. Walk away with a strip.

What it is

Not a rental trailer. A permanent wall fixture that prints the same glossy strips wedding booths hand out.

tiny photo booth mounts to the studs, glows with a soft perimeter light, and prints classic glossy 2×6" four-frame photo strips on a dye-sublimation printer — real photo paper, cut into keepsakes, no attendant.

Same strip format as event booths: four poses stacked on a 2×6, often two copies from one 4×6 sheet. Built for dive bars instead of ballrooms — small footprint, one-button flow, earns on the wall without eating floor space.

Guest price is a flat $5. One illuminated Start button, a QR code on the screen to pay by phone, a short countdown, four shots, and a cut strip in hand.

  1. Idle screen shows sample strips and $5 — Press Start
  2. Screen shows a QR code; guest scans and pays on their phone
  3. Photo light comes up; three-second countdown
  4. Four frames → dual 2×6 layout → dye-sub print and cut

The smallest stack that can survive real bar use — not just a demo on a workbench.

Same print tech wedding booths use: dye-sublimation photo paper and ribbon. Media runs about a few cents per strip once the printer is amortized.

How the money splits

The bar hosts the wall. The operator runs the fleet. Every strip is a clean split.

Guests pay $5. The venue keeps 30%. The operator keeps 70% and covers the hardware, paper, service, and card fees.

Thirty percent is what it takes to be worth a bar's wall. Jukebox and amusement operators land in the same range when they own and service the machine — the venue supplies space, power, and a staff member who can say “it's out of paper.”

Card processing (~2.9% + 30¢, about 45¢ on a $5 sale paid by phone) and one 4×6 dye-sub sheet (~16¢, cut into two strips) come out of the operator side — roughly $2.90 net per session. Moving to a tap reader later cuts the fee to ~19¢ and lifts that to $3.15.
