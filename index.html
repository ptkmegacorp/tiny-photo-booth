tiny photo booth

A wall-mounted photo strip machine for dive bars. Press start. Pay five bucks. Walk away with a strip.

What it is

Not a rental trailer. Not a wedding booth. A permanent fixture that earns quietly on the wall.

tiny photo booth mounts to the studs, glows with a soft perimeter light, and prints classic black-and-white four-frame strips on thermal paper — roughly 2.8 × 8 inches, cut automatically, no ink, no attendant.

It matches the dive-bar aesthetic on purpose: grainy, high-contrast, a little newspaper, a little old-school booth. Guests get a physical souvenir for the night; the bar gets something that doesn’t eat floor space or need a staffer to run it.

Guest price is a flat $5. One illuminated Start button, a certified card reader for tap / chip / swipe, a short countdown, four shots, and a cut strip in hand.

  1. Idle screen shows sample strips and $5 — Press Start
  2. Guest pays on the Stripe reader (tap, chip, or swipe)
  3. Photo light comes up; three-second countdown
  4. Four frames, composed into a strip, printed and cut

V1 build manifest

The smallest stack that can survive real bar use — not just a demo on a workbench.

Part Choice
Computer Raspberry Pi 5 (4 GB) — preview, compose, print, kiosk UI
Camera Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 — 12 MP, autofocus
Display 5–7" non-touch HDMI — countdown & instructions only
Printer Epson TM-T20 (USB, auto-cutter, 80 mm thermal)
Payment Stripe Reader S700 — separate certified device, not DIY NFC
Controls Large illuminated Start + smaller Cancel
Lighting Bright photo LEDs + decorative perimeter strip (Pi-switched)
Enclosure ~13" W × 21–23" H × 8–10" D, wall plate behind printed shell
Build cost ~$680–1,060 deployed · ~$350–650 bench prototype

Thermal is the V1 compromise: fast, cheap media, auto-cut, and a look that fits the bar. If the machine proves people will pay, a dye-sub printer can replace it later without redesigning the rest of the system.

How the money splits

The bar hosts the wall. The operator runs the fleet. Every strip is a clean split.

Guests pay $5. The venue keeps 15%. The operator keeps 85% and covers paper, service, and card fees.
